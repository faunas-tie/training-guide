Warm-Up — ~10 min

Work through in order. Single-leg work later asks for balance and right-side control — prime the ankle, hip and glute well.

01
Ankle circles — 10 each direction, each foot. First, given the Achilles history. Extra time if the right heel feels stiff.
02
Glute bridges — 2 × 10. Drive hips up, squeeze hard at the top, lower slowly. Wakes the posterior chain.
03
Hip circles / single-leg balance — 10 per leg, or 30 s balance per leg. Opens the hip; rehearses single-leg stance.
04
Bodyweight squats — 10 slow, parallel or below. Rehearses the knee-extension pattern the Leg Press loads.
05
Leg swings (front-back) — 10 per leg. Loosen hamstrings and hip flexors.
06
Calf raises (bodyweight) — 2 × 8 slow, 3 s down. Prep for the calf work; extra 30 s if the right Achilles feels tight.
07
Dead bug (unloaded) — 5 per side. Feel the lower back press flat into the floor.
Exercises
01 / 05
Leg Press
Life Fitness Optima · OSLP · 3 × 8 @ 65 kg · Rest 90–120 sec
Seated horizontal press — quads first, glutes and hamstrings assisting, no spinal load.
Quick reset
  • Back flush; knees ~90° at start; whole foot on the platform; latches engaged.
Tap when done
RepsLoad
RIR
Key cues
  • Push the floor away — glutes pinned to the seat, don't lift the hips.
  • Knees track over the toes — don't let the right knee cave inward under fatigue. That's your old collapse pattern; the late reps are where it shows.
  • Soft lockout, 2 s back.
65 kg is the working target. If set 1 sits at RPE ≤5, hold today and we step up next session; if form breaks or the right knee caves, drop a pin. Log the weight + RPE.
02 / 05
Single-Leg Leg Press
Life Fitness Optima · OSLP · 3 × 8/leg @ 30 kg/leg · Rest 90 sec per leg
Unilateral pressing on the leg press sled. Doubles the effective resistance available from a 100 kg stack, and carries the loadable unilateral work that the 11–19 kg dumbbell gap makes awkward to progress with free weights.
Quick reset
  • Foot centred on the platform; back flush; don't twist; latches engaged.
Tap when done
RepsLoad
RIR
Key cues
  • Knee tracks over the second toe. This is the exercise where your right knee collapse will show — if it caves, that set is over.
  • Hips square to the platform; don't let the pelvis rotate to help.
  • Lead with the right leg each session so it gets the fresh effort, and match the left to whatever the right managed.
Right knee collapse and hip drop under fatigue is your documented injury pattern (2021 groin/hip, 2022 Achilles). Unilateral pressing exposes it more than any other movement here. Watch the last two reps of every set — if the knee tracks inward, stop the set rather than finishing it.
30 kg/leg is per leg. New movement — open well below half your bilateral load and build. Recalibrate after set 1 on the right.
03 / 05
Romanian Deadlift
Dumbbells · 3 × 10 @ 10 kg/hand · Rest 90 sec
Hip-hinge loading the hamstrings and glutes eccentrically. Strong hamstrings offload the calf and Achilles — one of the most tendon-protective lifts here. Primary hinge for the block. Pairs to 10 kg/hand in 1 kg steps, plus a single 20 kg pair; the 11–19 kg gap is bridged by B-stance (front leg takes ~80% of the load), not by an intermediate pair.
Quick reset
  • Dumbbell each hand at the hips; soft knees.
Tap when done
RepsLoad
RIR
Key cues
  • Hips back, not down — if the knees bend a lot, you're squatting.
  • Long spine — neutral back, don't round the upper back.
  • 2–3 s down — the slow eccentric is the point.
The RDL lengthens the calf complex under load. Slow, controlled descent; a muscle stretch is fine, anything sharp in the tendon is a stop signal — log it.
04 / 05
Lying Leg Curl
Life Fitness Optima · OSLEC · 3 × 15 @ 14 kg · Rest 90 sec
Isolated knee-flexion hamstring work. Knee flexion is the hamstring's other function — it does not replace the hinge, and the RDL does not replace this.
Quick reset
  • Face down, hips flat; knee on the pivot; ankle pad set high enough that it finishes above the heel, not on the Achilles.
Tap when done
RepsLoad
RIR
Key cues
  • Slow the way back down — hamstrings respond to the lengthened, loaded position.
  • Hips stay pinned to the pad; if they lift, the load is too heavy.
  • Toes pulled up toward the shins, not pointed — stops the calf assisting.
  • Stop just short of straight at the bottom to hold tension.
The ankle roller travels toward the heel through the rep. Set it a hole shorter than feels natural so it terminates on the lower calf, not the tendon. If it still creeps onto the Achilles at peak flexion, shorten the range — losing 10° is the cheaper trade given your 2022 history.
14 kg is the working target. New movement — recalibrate after set 1.
05 / 05
Copenhagen Plank
Bodyweight · 3 × 30 s/side BW · Rest 90 sec
Side-plank loading the hip adductors under a long lever — one of the best-evidenced groin-stability exercises for runners, and direct carry-over from your 2021 rehab.
Quick reset
  • Forearm down, top foot/ankle on a bench; knee version if the hold gets sloppy.
Tap when done
RepsLoad
Key cues
  • Push the surface away — the active drive is what loads the adductor.
  • Stack the hips — squared hips keep the right side out of its collapse pattern.
  • Quality over the clock — a clean shorter hold beats a sagging long one.
Sharp groin/adductor pain or a notably weaker right side — shorten the hold or use the knee version, and log it.

06
Child's pose — 60 s. Finish here.
